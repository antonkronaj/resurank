Exclusion words are filtered out of both your resume and the pasted job description before any scoring happens. They never reach the embedding model or the TF-IDF index.

When to add a word
  • Industry filler that pollutes every job description ("responsibilities", "requirements", "team").
  • Recruiter boilerplate ("equal opportunity", "competitive salary", "benefits").
  • Terms that consistently rank high in the Raw counts view but have no signal value.
How it interacts with scoring

Removing a word lowers the noise floor — both texts shrink, so genuine matches make up a larger share of what remains. This usually increases good scores and decreases noisy ones.

A built-in stopword list (common English words) is always applied. Your exclusions are added on top.

Quick add

You don't need to open this list to add a word — click the − button next to any term in the keyword list to push it straight into your exclusions.

Term boosts multiply the TF-IDF weight of specific terms — useful when you want a particular skill to count for more than a generic word of the same frequency.

How a boost is applied

For every boosted term that appears in the job description, its TF-IDF weight is multiplied by the number you set. So java: 3 makes "java" contribute roughly 3× as much to the keyword half of your match score as a normal term of the same frequency.

Boosts only take effect for terms that appear in both your resume and the job description — you cannot conjure a match from nothing.

When to use boosts
  • To emphasise headline skills that you want any matching role to share.
  • To compensate for short resumes where a key term only appears once and gets a low TF.
  • To distinguish between similar terms — e.g. boost "kubernetes" if you don't want generic "cloud" to dominate.
Caveats
  • Boosts only affect the TF-IDF half of the score, not the embedding half.
  • Setting a weight too high can make borderline matches look better than they are. Start at 2 and adjust.
  • Use lowercase — terms are normalised before lookup.

Critical missing keywords reduce your match score when a term you've flagged appears in the job description but is absent from your resume. It's a one-sided penalty — flagged terms you do have add nothing; flagged terms you don't have take points away.

How the penalty is computed

Only flagged terms that appear in the current job description count. Each contributes a weight equal to its JD-side TF-IDF score multiplied by its importance tier.

penalty = (missing_weight ÷ total_weight) × max_reduction

The Max reduction slider below sets the max_reduction term directly. Example: if half of your flagged terms (by weight) are missing and the slider is at 25%, the final score drops by 12.5 percentage points.

Importance tiers
  • Low — 0.5× weight. Nice-to-have terms.
  • Medium (default) — 1× weight.
  • High — 2× weight. Deal-breaker terms.

Tiers are symmetric around medium, so a "medium" flag behaves identically to an unweighted flag.

Max reduction

Caps how much the penalty can drop the score. Defaults to 25% and can go up to 50%. A JD where every flagged term is missing hits the cap; partial misses scale proportionally.

Quick add

You don't have to type terms here — click the ⚑ button next to any keyword in the JD keywords panel to flag it inline. The same list applies to every job description you score.
