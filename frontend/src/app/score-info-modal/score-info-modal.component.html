How scoring works

ResuRank scores a job description against your resume using two independent methods — semantic embedding and keyword TF-IDF — then combines them into a single percentage.

Step 1 — Text preparation
  • Stopwords are removed. Common words and any words in your personal exclusion list are stripped out.
  • The job title gets extra weight. It is repeated twice before the description so title terms count more.
  • Text is sanitised for the embedding model. HTML, URLs, emoji, and Markdown are stripped before sending to the AI model.
  • Inputs are capped at 32,000 characters for the UI counter, and 6,000 characters (after sanitisation) before the embedding model.
Step 2 — Embedding score (semantic similarity)

Answers: do these two texts mean the same thing, even if they use different words?

Both texts are passed through a local AI model (jina-embeddings-v2-small-en, ~25 MB, runs fully offline). The model converts each text into a vector representing its meaning. The score is the cosine similarity between the two vectors.

Good at catching paraphrases ("led a team" ↔ "people management"), but can find abstract similarity even when texts share no keywords — which is why it is not used alone.

Step 3 — TF-IDF score (keyword similarity)

Answers: do these two texts share the same specific words?

Builds a two-document keyword index and computes cosine similarity in keyword-weight space. Rare terms are weighted more than common ones ("Kubernetes" matters more than "experience").

  • Overlap bonus: up to +20 percentage points when your top 100 resume terms appear in the job description.
  • Term boosts: configured boost terms get their TF-IDF weight multiplied (only effective for terms already in your resume).
Step 4 — Combining the scores
score = 0.60 × embedding + 0.40 × TF-IDF

The embedding gets more weight because it captures meaning. The TF-IDF anchors the score to actual shared vocabulary.

Step 5 — Divergence adjustment

The embedding can find semantic similarity between any two professional texts even with zero keyword overlap. To correct for this, the embedding weight is smoothly reduced as TF-IDF approaches zero:

  • TF-IDF ≥ 15% — normal weights (60/40). No adjustment.
  • TF-IDF near 0% — embedding drops to 10%, TF-IDF rises to 90%. Collapses the score toward near-zero TF-IDF.
  • In between — smooth linear transition.

The "Divergence penalty" in the breakdown is the difference between the normal-weight score and the adjusted score.

Language detection

If more than 3% of alphabetic characters in the job description are non-ASCII, a warning is shown. The divergence adjustment also helps here since TF-IDF will typically be near zero for a foreign-language job.
