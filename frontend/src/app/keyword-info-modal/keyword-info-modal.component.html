The weighted view ranks each term in the job description by its TF-IDF weight — a measure of how distinctive the term is, not just how often it appears.

How the weight is calculated

Each term's weight combines two factors:

  • Term frequency (TF): how often the term appears in the job description.
  • Inverse document frequency (IDF): rarer terms across the resume + job pair get a higher boost. Common words like "experience" score low; specific terms like "Kubernetes" score high.

The bar length is relative to the top-ranked term. Any boosts you have configured are also applied here.

Why it matters

This is the view that drives the TF-IDF component of your match score. Highlighted rows are terms that also appear in your resume — those are the keywords actually contributing to the overlap bonus.

The raw counts view shows how many times each term literally appears in the job description, after stopwords and your exclusions are removed.

How it differs from weighted

No IDF, no boosts — just occurrence counts. A common word that appears 10 times will rank above a rare, distinctive term that appears once.

When to use it
  • To spot terms the job description repeats — often a signal of what the role really emphasises.
  • To find noisy filler terms worth adding to your exclusions so they stop polluting the weighted view.

Raw counts do not directly affect your match score; they are a diagnostic view.
